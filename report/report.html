1. What we chose not to do, and why

  • One shape band, one start pose. A four-hour budget. Getting the full pipeline honest on one band beats a wide envelope we cannot measure. The wider envelope is swept in eval only (§6).
  • No learned grasp. The grasp is a script. A learned grasp is the obvious next step. Its reward cannot see T* (it is hidden until lift), so the right objective is the reorient critic's value averaged over the target prior. That is implemented (plan A in the repo) but not trained here.
  • No perception stack. Ground-truth pose stands in for vision, gated by visibility (§2).
  • Plans A, B, C in the repo are not the submission. They share this core but were not trained to convergence. Only the scripted-grasp + learned-reorient system (PlanS) is evaluated.

3. Decomposition and stage interfaces

Stage 1: scripted grasp (inhand/planc/scripted_grasp.py). It reads only the deploy observation. The object pose comes from the gated vision stand-in, which sees the rod on the floor.

  1. IK to a palm-down pre-grasp above the rod, fingers across its axis, rod 5 cm toward the fingertips from the palm origin.
  2. Descend until the palm is 2 cm into the rod top, which pins it to the floor.
  3. Close the fingers over 12 steps.
  4. Catch: if the lift flag flips during the close (the rod popped), finish the close and rise at 30 cm/s.
  5. Lift 15 cm at 10 cm/s, then roll the wrist to palm-up in joint space.

It never reads T*. It uses the lift flag only as an event.

Stage 2: learned reorient (PlanS in inhand/systems.py). A recurrent PPO actor on the deploy observation, running from handover to the end of the episode. The interface between the stages is the simulator state itself: the policy is trained from a bank of 266 states saved at real handovers (scripts/collect_handovers.py), mixed 70/30 with synthetic in-hand drops for coverage.

4. MDP of the learned component

ObservationActor: the 161-d deploy vector above, with time measured from handover. Critic: deploy vector + object pose/velocity in the palm frame, r, h, mass, frictions, contact flags, true target (asymmetric actor-critic, train only).
Action16 finger delta-targets in [−1, 1] × 0.1 rad. Arm actions are zeroed: the target is palm-frame, and moving the arm only adds ways to touch the object with an arm link.
Rewardexp(−epos/3 cm) + exp(−eang/0.4 rad) + 2·[in tolerance] + 50·[success] − 10·[drop or violation] − 0.002‖a‖². All shaping is positive and bounded. With negative per-step error the first policies learned that ending the episode early (throwing the rod into the arm) was optimal.
TerminationAs §2, 10 s horizon (the grasp uses about 9 s of the 20 s episode).
AlgorithmPPO, GRU(256) over MLP(512, 256, 128). γ 0.99, λ 0.95, clip 0.2, 4 epochs × 4 minibatches, KL-adaptive LR (target 0.02), 56 envs × 64 steps per iteration.
WhyThe fingers occlude the rod, so pose must be carried through time from tactile and proprioception: hence a recurrent actor. Privileged state makes the value function learnable in minutes rather than hours. Training directly on deploy observations removes the teacher–student distillation step, which the time budget could not afford.

5. The grasp stage: what it costs and what fixed it

The grasp decides both whether the episode gets to reorientation and where the object starts in the hand. Rates below are handovers (object hand-only after the palm-up roll) on lying rods of the trained band.

Trade-off. A scripted grasp took about 40 minutes to reach 55 % and needs no training, but it caps end-to-end success at 55 %. It also hands over a spread of in-hand poses (axis 2°–59° from palm y, 10th–90th percentile) that the reorient policy must absorb. A learned, value-aware grasp could both lift more reliably and pick grasps that are easy to reorient from. That is the single change with the largest expected gain.

8. Limitations and next steps

  • Grasp is the ceiling. 45 % of episodes never reach reorientation. Next: train the value-aware grasp (plan A) on the 2 ms physics, warm-started from the script by DAgger.
  • Narrow band. One shape band and one start pose. Next: widen r and h with ADR once the grasp is learned. Standing starts need either a tip-over or a side grasp.
  • Target prior centred on the grasp's typical pose. A defensible feasibility choice, but the policy only has to tilt ≤ 40° and move a few cm. Next: widen the cone with the existing curriculum.
  • Compute. Single laptop, CPU MuJoCo. MJX or MuJoCo Warp would give roughly 100× the environment steps.
  • Vision stand-in. Ground truth when ≥ 30 % visible. A real estimator (e.g. FoundationPose) would add latency and error that we have not modelled.
